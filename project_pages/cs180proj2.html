Part 2.1: Image "Sharpening"

Imagine that a blurry image is made up of both high and low frequencies. The low frequencies make up the blurry parts of the image, while the high makes up the sharpened parts. This part of the project goes about sharpening our images by subtracting a blur mask (which we create by convolving our image with a 2d Gaussian kernel) from the original image. This takes away all of the blurry low frequencies, leaving us a new, unsharp mask. This unsharp mask are our isolated high frequencies. When this unsharp mask is added to our original blurry image, all of the high frequencies are emphasized, leaving us with a more clear, sharp image!

Looking at our images, we can observe the sharpening of our original Taj Mahal and cat images, and the high frequency filters that did the sharpening. The waterfall portion is a special case, because it was an already-sharpened image. It underwent a blurring, and re-sharpening with the pictured high frequency filter. The subsequently re-sharpened picture seems to be more sharp than the original, with the lighting contrats more exacperated.
