Part 1.1B Training a Single-Step Denoising UNet - Implementing the UNet

The goal of part B of this project was to build and train a flow matching model on photos of MNIST digits (handwritten, greyscale images of numbers 0-9 that are a standard ML training/testing benchmark dataset). In short, the goal of our model is to take noisy images of digits, and decipher them into clean images of digits.

The first section of part B involved building a baseline, one-step denoiser, UNet. This UNet is made up of a multiple of blocks, with each block involving its own series of torch operations. I built these block operators [Conv, DownConv, UpConv, Flatten, Unflatten] first, then used said blocks in the creation of the model. Building the model wasn't dissimilar to what it was like building the NeRF model from the Project 4. However there were extra degrees of complexity due to the blocks. I had to build the model with blocks that call torch operations, rather than using torch operations directly.

Part 1.2.3B Denoising Pure Noise

For this part, rather than train a model to denoise noisy-digit images, I'm training a model to denoise pure noise. Below, I have displayed the training loss curve plot, and the sample results for epoch 1 and 5.

Within my generated sample results, all of the model's outputs looks the same across the first and last epoch. The output looks like a combination of blurred MNIST digits. My explanation as to why this occurs has to do with how the model predicts points that minimize the sum of squared distances (aka the amount of difference) from all training examples. Because the model is given pure noise, the minimized sum is the mean of all the digit images in the training set.
